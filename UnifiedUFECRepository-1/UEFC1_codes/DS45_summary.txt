DS.4

For DS.4, the fastest design in the scanned design space occurred at
lambda = 0.50 and tau = 0.12, while the slowest design occurred at
lambda = 1.00 and tau = 0.08.

After the refined scan, the best case achieved a flight speed of
V = 11.09 m/s at approximately AR = 7.75 and S = 0.1535 m^2.
The worst case achieved a flight speed of V = 10.25 m/s at
approximately AR = 4.00 and S = 0.1805 m^2.

The best DS.4 design uses a more tapered and thicker wing, along with
a higher aspect ratio and smaller wing area. This combination reduces
induced drag and improves speed. In contrast, the worst DS.4 design
uses a more rectangular and thinner wing, along with a lower aspect
ratio and larger wing area, which increases drag and reduces
performance.

The best design lies near the active thrust, lift, and wing-tip
deflection constraints, showing that the optimizer pushes the airplane
to the edge of the feasible design space in order to maximize speed.

DS.5

For DS.5, the fastest design also occurred at lambda = 0.50 and
tau = 0.12, while the slowest design occurred at lambda = 1.00 and
tau = 0.08.

After the refined scan, the best DS.5 case achieved a flight speed of
V = 11.18 m/s at approximately AR = 8.00 and S = 0.1355 m^2.
The worst DS.5 case achieved a flight speed of V = 10.36 m/s at
approximately AR = 4.25 and S = 0.1745 m^2.

Compared with DS.4, the relaxed constraints in DS.5
((d/b)_max = 0.10 and CL_des = 0.90) allow slightly higher speeds,
higher aspect ratios, and smaller wing areas for the best-performing
designs.

The best DS.5 designs are still primarily limited by thrust and
wing-tip deflection. Overall, the DS.5 results show the same general
trend as DS.4: lower lambda, higher tau, higher AR, and lower S
produce the fastest airplanes, while higher lambda, lower tau,
lower AR, and larger S produce the slowest ones.
